## Strategy Guide

### Philosophy

In order to finish Impossible Mission, you may need to change your mindset of the game itself.  While Impossible Mission may seem like a game about robots, fantastic somersaults, and puzzle pieces, there is one key to the game that is most important above all else.  Impossible Mission is about **time management**.  This may seem ridiculous to the novice player; lives feel generously given and the game seems to go on for at least a good half hour uninterrupted, even with frequent mistakes.  But, that is all deception. In the background, Impossible Mission tracks every little mistake.  Over time, theycompound and can completely ruin your chance to finish the game.  Perhaps this is better explained with some statistics:

 * You have 6 hours to complete the game (between noon at 6pm).
 * Each time you fall to your death, you lose 10 minutes
 * Each time you touch a robot or floating orb, you lose 10 minutes
 * Each use of the Phone costs 2 minutes of game time.  But, in reality, it costs more as you wait for the phone to ring, etc...  The real cost is 2 minutes and 12 seconds.

Let's look at this a little more closely.  6 hours translates to 360 minutes.  And, each death costs you 10 minutes.  To make it easy, this means in a game where time stood still, you only would be allowed a maximum of 36 deaths.

This should give the avid player something to think about.  Even if it takes 9 minutes to clear a room by being slow and very, very careful, this is still better than a single death.  It means that you should really take your time.  Deaths are costly and should be treated as such.

Next, we should account for other variables just to see how time-bound we really are.

If a player gets all 36 puzzle pieces and doesn't want to waste time with orienting them, they can use the Phone to reorient pieces two at a time.  This translates to 18 phone calls.  Each phone call costs 2 minutes and 12 seconds, but we need to adjust the pieces in view between calls, so we should add another 8 seconds to account for this.  This means each call will cost us 2 minutes and 20 seconds.  In total (when accounting for 18 calls), this adds up to 42 minutes.  That's a precious amount of time taken when you only have 6 hours.  From here, if it takes you 18 minutes to assemble the 9 puncards from the pieces together, that's an hour invested.  This leaves only 5 hours left for game-play.  And, given that there are 32 rooms, that leaves under 10 minutes per room.  Which, over the long haul is less than one death per room to be successful.

**Conclusion:** This is a game about patience.  Going into a room, finding a safe path through the robots, and incrementally whittling down a piece of furniture until the room is clear is a much better strategy than a haphazard "well, everything will reset anyway" aproach.  If you combine this idea with the fact that some rooms require pixel-perfect jumps to get to certain furniture pieces and that robots have randomly set difficulties per playthrough, you will eventually realize that searching all furniture in all rooms up-front is a bad idea.  Instead, clear the easy rooms and easy furniture first.  Go back for the more difficult ones later.

**Remember:** there are only 36 puzzle pieces scattered throughout 126 pieces of furniture.  That makes an expected average of 2 puzzle pieces found for every 7 furniture items.  You don't need to search everything.  You just need to get to 36 puzzle pieces before you're done with the rooms in the game.
